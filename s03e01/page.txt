With your harness, turn it into one requirements list, then build the page from that list. Not all of it reaches your harness: some lives on the Challenge page.

THE TASK

You turned a pack of mixed files from several people into one cited requirements list, flagged where they disagreed instead of picking a side, and built the status page from that list.

WHAT HAPPENED

Six people's files had to become one cited requirements list, then a public status page built from it. What decided the solve was reading each source with its own reader, because a rule drawn on a screen never appears in a spoken transcript. Your part was the course sign-in and the call to publish on the Firebase site you already had.

Avoided: The board's signed link was masked out of the prompt sent to the reviewing model, so that token never left the course.
WHAT THIS CHALLENGE SHOWED

The board draws it WENT WRONG FIRST

Your harness fetched the board's image file first and got a scrambled tile sheet, so it opened the board in the browser it drives and read the canvas the page draws, which is where the rule about the boards sat.

Why it matters: What a page draws is not in its text or code. To read a live interface, render it and look.

Said is not shown WENT WRONG FIRST

The recording transcribed to one long block and never spoke the footer rule, so your harness paused it at chosen seconds and captured the notes panel, where all times UTC appeared at 23 seconds.

Why it matters: A transcript is half a video. Frames carry what nobody says out loud.

Neither side wins by rank WENT WRONG FIRST

Your harness shipped last night as a calendar day, so the course refused the page until the night shift's night was used; the date-format question stayed open for its owner rather than being decided.

Why it matters: Clear is not consistent. A conflict goes back to its owner as a question, never settled by a guess.

The row with no author WENT RIGHT THE FIRST TIME

Your harness recorded it as a conflict against the record itself, so the page shows an em dash there and the question of who ran it went to product instead of being invented.

Why it matters: A requirement the data cannot meet is a question for its owner, not a placeholder in the build.

Accepted is a contract WENT WRONG FIRST

The first page passed your harness's own checks and still failed the course, because the colour, the footer words and an older incident were missing; a verifier now asserts each accepted tag against the live address.

Why it matters: Outcome over output: the build delivers every accepted requirement, the quiet ones too.
