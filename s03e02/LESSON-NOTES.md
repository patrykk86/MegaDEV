## The lesson in one paragraph

An agent moves fast, but only where it thinks it is going: it has to be told where it is heading
without the operator answering every question or watching every move, and without losing quality or
the proper shape of the product. The lesson lays that out in four sections — intent, product context,
requirements and decomposition, evaluation — and argues the hard part is not speed but getting the
right work done, done right, in a real environment while still using what the model knows. Most of
those "new" problems, it says, are ones engineers have always had.

## The four sections, and what each asks of a session

- **Describing intent** — definition of done, user impact, existing behaviour, desired outcome. What it
  asks here: one written line for what the human can do once the work is finished.
- **Product context** — product and market knowledge, codebase maps, constraints, conventions, prior
  decisions. What it asks here: a map of the code and the decisions behind it, instead of re-deriving
  both every session.
- **Requirements and decomposition** — assumptions, ambiguities, contradictions and missing
  information, turned into focused questions and a structured brief with negative examples, boundaries,
  simplification, dependencies, checkpoints, rules and assignments. What it asks here: briefs that say
  what a job must not do, and one owner question per real disagreement.
- **Evaluation** — expectations, acceptance criteria, quality bars, evidence requirements, tests. What
  it asks here: the command, log line or file that proves the claim.

## Where it fell short, and what closed it

Both gaps sat in the lesson's first section, not its last three. Both are closed by a primitive rather
than by prose, because a rule nobody can run is a rule that drifts.

- **No standing brief for internal work** → `tools/brief.mjs`, its `brief.tool.json` schema and the
  `brief` skill. It requires the five blocks, refuses a brief whose proof count does not match its
  criteria, refuses a credential or an absolute path, and writes `.runs/<job>/brief.{json,md}`.
- **No codebase map** → `memory/CODEBASE-MAP.md`, generated by `tools/codebase-map.mjs` from the
  docblocks and local imports of `tools/`, every skill and every episode folder. The file carries a
  content fingerprint and `node tools/check.mjs` fails when it is stale, so it cannot rot.

## What we deliberately did not do

- **No hard policy file changed.** `AGENTS.md`, `.clinerules` and `.agents/rules/*` are untouched; the
  two new lines are soft rules in `memory/MEMORY.md`, the split S03E01 used for the same reason.
- **One new skill, not two.** `brief` carries the five blocks and the close-out procedure for work this
  session does itself; the lesson's middle section stays `crew`'s job, so nothing about delegation was
  duplicated. `codebase-map` got no skill: it is a generator with one deterministic output, and its
  schema records that as the `standaloneReason`.
- **No invented detail.** The reply truncated part of the lesson, so those parts are marked as a gap in
  `lesson-excerpt.md` rather than reconstructed from memory.
